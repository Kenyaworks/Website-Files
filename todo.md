Here's my feedback on the Kenya Works website design:
Strengths:
The design effectively communicates the organization's mission through a clear visual hierarchy and compelling storytelling. The hero section with Joy's story is emotionally engaging and immediately establishes the human impact of the organization's work. The four pillars (Shelter, Menstrual Health & Hygiene, Education, Community) are well-organized with consistent iconography that's easy to scan. The 2024 impact statistics provide credible, quantifiable outcomes that build donor confidence. The color scheme appears professional, and the navigation structure is logical with clear menu organization.
Areas for improvement:

Visual hierarchy: The navigation menu contains several nested items that could be more streamlined or reorganized. Some users might benefit from clearer visual distinction between main sections and sub-pages.
Call-to-action prominence: While "Donate" and "Sponsor" buttons are present, you might consider making the primary CTA (whether that's donation or engagement) more visually prominent throughout the page. Currently, they're somewhat integrated into the navigation.
Mobile responsiveness: From the fetched HTML structure, I can't fully assess mobile layout, but given that nonprofits see significant mobile traffic from donors, ensuring the design scales well (especially the hero image and impact statistics) is critical.
Content density: The page is information-rich, which is good for conveying impact, but breaking up longer sections with more visual elements or whitespace might improve readability and prevent overwhelming first-time visitors.
Accessibility: Ensure all images have descriptive alt text (especially the impact icons and Joy's photo), and check color contrast ratios for text readability.
Trust signals: The four-star charity rating badge at the bottom is great—consider promoting that earlier on the page where donors might see it before scrolling.
