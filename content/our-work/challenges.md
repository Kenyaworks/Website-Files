## Why This Work Matters

In Kenya, poverty, early marriage, female genital mutilation, period poverty, and gender-based violence continue to limit the futures of children and families. These issues are deeply connected, and they often overlap with barriers to education, safety, dignity, and opportunity. Kenya Works partners with communities to confront these challenges through education, advocacy, and locally led action.

## Poverty

Poverty shapes nearly every part of daily life, from what families eat to whether children can stay in school. In Kenya, about 40% of people live below the national poverty line and about 32% are food poor.  
[Learn more about poverty in Kenya →](/our-work/poverty/)

## Early Marriage

Early marriage can end a child’s future before it has fully begun. It is driven by poverty, low education, and social pressure, and it puts children’s health, safety, and future at risk.  
[Learn more about early marriage →](/our-work/early-marriage/)

## Female Genital Mutilation

FGM is a harmful practice with no medical benefit and lasting physical and emotional consequences. It remains deeply rooted in some communities, which is why prevention must be community-based and culturally informed.  
[Learn more about FGM in Kenya →](/our-work/female-genital-mutilation/)

## Period Poverty

Period poverty keeps too many girls from participating fully in school, work, and daily life. About 65% of girls and women in Kenya cannot afford sanitary pads, which can lead to missed school, health risks, and stigma.  
[Learn more about period poverty →](/our-work/period-poverty/)

## Gender-Based Violence

Gender-based violence threatens the safety, health, and future of women, children, and families. In Kenya, over 40% of women have experienced intimate partner violence, and stronger prevention, protection, and support for survivors are urgently needed.  
[Learn more about gender-based violence →](/our-work/gender-based-violence/)

## A Kenyan Economy Expanding, but Unequal

Kenya has recently achieved lower-middle income status, with a GDP of roughly $95 billion. Extreme poverty fell from 46.8% in 2005 to 36.1% in 2015 — real progress. But growth has not been shared evenly: two-thirds of Kenyans live in poverty, earning less than $3.20 a day, and the majority face chronic vulnerability, especially women and girls.

## Informal Settlements: A Snapshot

Rapid urbanization has pushed families into informal settlements with insecure residential status and severe overcrowding — in Nairobi, roughly 60% of the city's 4.7 million residents occupy just 5% of its footprint. Housing is often built from mud or metal sheeting, without reliable access to water, electricity, or sewage. That inequality compounds into conflict, crime, substance abuse, domestic abuse, and child endangerment.

## The Rural Divide: A Snapshot

Rural poverty declined from 49.7% in 2005/06 to 37.0% in 2019, while urban poverty dropped from 34.5% to 26.0% over the same period — yet most of Kenya's poor still live in rural areas, and women and girls face the most severe disadvantages. Key drivers include:

- Limited access to quality education beyond the primary level
- Inequality in accessing economic and social goods and services
- Unfavorable climate conditions, including droughts that devastate agriculture
- Insufficient job opportunities and underdeveloped infrastructure
- Large family sizes straining household resources

## The Gender Impact of Rural Poverty

Women make up over 60% of Kenya's rural population, and absolute poverty among rural women stands at 49.1%. 44% of all Kenyan women are poor in at least one dimension, and 95% of multi-dimensionally poor women live in rural areas. Rural women face compounding barriers:

- Limited access to political, economic, and social opportunities
- Less likely to own assets or access productive resources
- Educational barriers — rural girls are less likely to transition to secondary school
- Disproportionate unpaid labor, including childcare and household work
- Greater vulnerability to climate shocks and economic downturns

This is the need our four programs — Shelter Works, Education Works, MHH Works, and Community Works — were built to address.
