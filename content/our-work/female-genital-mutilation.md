## Female Genital Mutilation

Female genital mutilation is a violation of girls’ rights and has no medical justification. It involves the partial or total removal of external female genitalia or other injury for non-medical reasons. In Kenya, the practice has declined over time, but it remains deeply rooted in some communities and continues to harm girls physically, emotionally, and socially.

FGM is often justified as a cultural rite of passage or linked to marriageability, but its consequences are severe. It can cause pain, infection, complications in childbirth, trauma, and long-term health problems. It is also closely tied to other forms of violence, including child marriage and denial of education.

Progress is possible when communities lead the change. National efforts, education, local advocacy, and survivor-centered approaches have helped reduce prevalence, but prevention must continue, especially where girls remain at highest risk. Community-based action is essential because harmful norms are sustained locally and must be transformed locally.

## Kenya Works Response

Kenya Works supports efforts to end FGM by working with communities to protect children, promote education, and challenge harmful social norms. We also support alternative rites of passage where appropriate, helping communities preserve meaningful traditions while replacing harm with education, celebration, and girls’ rights.
